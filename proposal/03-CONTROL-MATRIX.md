# Control framework: what each control partner will ask, and the evidence

A bank can't run a new tool until every control partner has signed off. This table answers them before they ask. **Gaps are stated, not hidden.** The prototype is a demo on synthetic data; production would run inside the bank's environment.

| Control partner | Governing expectation | What they will ask | Evidence today | Gap and plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Model Risk Management** | OCC Bulletin 2011-12 / Fed SR 11-7 (model risk management) | Is it a model? What tier? Conceptual soundness, outcome testing, ongoing monitoring. What about the LLM? | Verdicts come from written rules, not a fitted model. Every threshold was checked against 28 real public loan pools (`proof/REAL_DATA.md`), with tests that fail if a threshold moves into the honest range. Held-out test scored once (`proof/REPORT.md`). A switch-off test per check. Replay: 120 of 120 decisions identical. The agent never decides, and code drops its unsupported lines. | Statistical checks (roll-forward, score edge) make it likely **in scope as a model**: register it, tier it, and validate it independently on bank data before production. The LLM goes under the bank's AI-use policy. Monitoring plan: monthly catch and review-load report. |
| **First line: NDFI credit (business owner)** | Credit policy; delegated authority | Does it change who decides? Do the actions fit policy? | The officer approves every step; the tool never acts. Fix playbooks per trick. | Map each playbook step to Wells Fargo credit policy and approval limits. |
| **Operational Risk** | Operational-risk framework (process, people, systems) | What fails, and what happens then? Key-person risk? | Each check runs isolated with a time budget. Bad files are quarantined and the last good copy is used. Each case says which checks didn't run. Fallback tests in `tests/`. | Risk-and-control self-assessment entry, run-book, service levels, on-call. |
| **Compliance: financial crimes (BSA/AML)** | Bank Secrecy Act; suspicious activity reporting, 12 CFR 21.11; beneficial ownership, 31 CFR 1010.230. Heightened focus: Wells Fargo's 2024 OCC formal agreement covers BSA/AML, including SAR filing and beneficial ownership | Does a "likely fraud" finding trigger a SAR decision? Is the clock respected? Could letters tip off the borrower? | Evidence chains with a source for every clue. Shells linked by owner, address, phone and registered agent. Full audit trail. | **Route every likely-fraud case to the financial-crimes unit** for a SAR decision within the regulatory timeline. Borrower letters must never reveal suspicion or a SAR (tipping-off): Compliance and Legal approve the templates. |
| **Legal** | Contract rights; defamation; data-use terms | Can we request this data? Could a letter defame? Can public data be used this way? | Letters ask the borrower to explain the evidence and never accuse. Public data sources documented (SEC EDGAR fair-access rules; GLEIF open data). | Confirm information and audit rights in each credit agreement. Legal review of all templates. Data vendor license review. |
| **Information Security / Technology Risk** | Interagency guidelines establishing information security standards (GLBA) | Data classification (obligor details in tapes), access control, encryption, logging, hosting | No secrets in code or history. Security headers on the demo. The case service binds to localhost; writes from other machines need a key; input sizes capped (`SecurityTest.java`). The demo holds no real data. Open-weight models, so no data leaves the bank. | Bank SSO and entitlements, encryption at rest, deployment inside the bank's environment, SDLC review, penetration test. |
| **Third-Party Risk** | Interagency guidance on third-party relationships (2023) | Which vendors and components does it depend on? | Open-source stack (Python, DuckDB, Spring Boot, Next.js). Open-weight models (Qwen3). No required SaaS. | Third-party reviews for lien/court data vendors. Open-source license review. |
| **Data governance and privacy** | Bank data policies; GLBA | Minimum necessary data? Retention? Lineage? | Each input is fingerprinted and every decision names its inputs (decision record). | Data-use approval, retention schedule, lineage entries. |
| **Internal Audit** | Third line | Can we audit a decision end to end? | Decision record (inputs, rules, code, result fingerprints), replay tool, approval audit log. | Audit walk-through during the pilot. |

**Not applicable, and why that matters:** FINRA rules govern broker-dealers, and GDPR is EU privacy law. A US bank's commercial lending to non-bank lenders is governed by the regimes above. Naming the right ones is part of the credibility.

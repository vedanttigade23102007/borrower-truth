## Judgement (Claude, 1 Oct 2026)

**No sign that the red team saw the rules.**

- **The `DOUBLE_PLEDGE` and `round_trip` hits** are two attack names, `double_pledge` and `round_trip`. They are the DOJ First Brands release's own words: "Double- and Triple-Pledged Assets", and transfers known internally as "round trips". The red team quotes both in its READMEs.
- **The `DEFERMENT_PROGRAM` hit** is the data value `deferment_program`, from the table guide.
- **The numbers** are ordinary code literals: rounding to 2 places, 60-month default terms, the 20th percentile, and so on.

**Read by a person:**
- All 8 `common.py` copies are identical.
- No script imports `os`, `sys`, `subprocess`, `socket`, `urllib` or `shutil`, or calls `open`, `exec` or `eval`. They read and write tables only through `bookio`.
- **What the red team said it noticed and didn't use** (its README): the PledgeCheck column names, and the 0.15% background rate of tape-versus-servicer disagreement in the sample book.
- **What it did use:** eligibility follows the late-payment bucket. It argues that's the credit agreement's own definition, which a borrower knows.

**A realism point for the results, not a breach of the rules.** Three attacks record money as paid by someone other than the real sender:
- `round_trip`'s remittances come from a conduit company, but are recorded as paid by the real customer.
- `ghost_vins` and `abs_overlap` record payments from invented customers.
- `month_end_cure` records cure payments under the customers' own IDs, sent through a "payment processor".

The briefing allowed adding payments and didn't say whose name they may carry, so these plants stand. In a real collection account, the payer recorded is normally the actual sender, so these levers are stronger than most borrowers have.

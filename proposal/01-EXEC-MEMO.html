Catch borrower fraud in Wells Fargo's largest loan category months before a quarterly audit, using tools the bank already backs.

Decision requested: a read-only pilot on 20–40 auto-finance warehouse borrowers. About 19 FTE-months (~$0.5–1.0M planning range). No automated actions, no customer-facing change. Go / no-go returns to committee in 90 days.

Why now

  • Loans to non-bank financial firms are Wells Fargo's largest commercial category: $208.8B, 20% of all loans (10-Q, June 2026). Q1 2026 charge-offs rose "driven by a single fraud-related loss" (10-Q).
  • The 2025–26 frauds in this market (Tricolor, First Brands; DOJ and SEC allegations) each used several tricks at once, hidden in different places. Publicly described controls (field exams, collateral checks) each look in one place, often a sample at a time. After Q1, management said teams reviewed clients' procedures and collateral (earnings call); this makes that review monthly and automatic.

What it is

Seven checks, one per documented trick, joined into one case per borrower: what's wrong, the evidence chain, a suggested fix and a drafted letter. An officer approves every step. Verdicts come from written rules, not a model; every decision can be replayed. It uses Setpoint PledgeCheck results rather than replacing them. Its models are open-weight and can run inside the bank, so borrower data need not leave it.

Evidence so far (prototype)

TestResult
Fresh synthetic books, scored once (600 borrowers, 250 planted frauds)248 of 250 caught vs 38% for a quarterly sample audit; 0 of 350 honest borrowers flagged; a median 90 days earlier
Real public data: 2.2M SEC loan records, 28 pools, 22 lendersThe five loan-tape checks tested on every pool: 0 of 28 honest pools flagged. The other checks can't be tested on public data yet
Workload29 of every 100 honest borrowers sent to "worth a look"; the top tenth of the queue was 60 of 60 real frauds

The frauds are planted, so these prove coverage of documented tricks, not a real-world catch rate. The pilot measures the real one.

Money

Break-even, not a forecast: moving the response to one ~$8M exposure three months earlier pays for the pilot, at 3% monthly erosion. Other rates and lead times are in the business case, and the bank's own loss data replaces these inputs.

Risk and controls

  • Model risk: likely in scope as a model. Register and tier it, and validate it independently before production. The LLM never decides.
  • BSA/AML: likely-fraud cases route to the financial-crimes unit for a SAR decision. Compliance and Legal approve the letter templates, so no letter tips off a borrower.
  • InfoSec and third parties: runs inside the bank, with bank SSO and encryption at rest (to build). Lien and court data vendors go through third-party review.

Next steps if approved

Weeks 0–4: data access, model-risk tiering, InfoSec review. Weeks 5–16: shadow pilot with weekly officer review. Week 17: go / no-go paper, with success and kill criteria agreed up front.
